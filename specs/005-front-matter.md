## Context

The front matter comprises modern essays and apparatus introducing Ruth Fuller Sasaki's translation and the historical transmission of the text:

1. **`foreword.md`** — Yamada Mumon Roshi (山田無文):
   Explains the spirit of Rinzai Zen, Chinese vs Indian Buddhism, Baizhang's "day of no work, day of no eating", and Nishida Kitarō's love of the *Linji lu*.
   *(Note: A preliminary translation draft already exists in `Record_of_Linji_Persian.md`)*.
2. **`preface-1975.md`** — Furuta Kazuhiro (古田和弘):
   Chronicles the thirty-year collaborative translation project at Ryōsen-an in Kyoto, Ruth Sasaki's sudden passing in 1967, and the publication of the 1975 first edition.
3. **`editors-prologue.md`** — Thomas Yūhō Kirchner:
   The longest and most detailed introductory essay. Contains:
   - Kirchner's own encounter with the text as a lay monk in Japan.
   - Comprehensive biographical sketch of Ruth Fuller Sasaki (Chicago socialite, yoga pioneer, first Westerner ordained as a Rinzai priest, Abbess of Ryōsen-an).
   - History of the 2008 Nanzan edition and archival recovery of the Ryōsen-an notebooks.
   - Acknowledgments.
4. **`abbreviations.md`**:
   Standard academic abbreviations for classical Chinese Buddhist canons (e.g., *Taishō Shinshū Daizōkyō* `t`, *Manji Shinsan Dainihon Zokuzōkyō* `x`, *Zen Dust* `zd`).
5. **`record-title-page.md`**:
   The formal bilingual title page of the *Linji lu*.

---

## Translation Strategy

- **Schedule Late**: Translate this group *after* the core body (Discourses and Critical Examinations) is completed. The prologue and prefaces reference key doctrinal concepts and names that must harmonize with the body.
- **Register**: Modern, eloquent scholarly Persian prose.
- **Images**: Preserve embedded images:
  - `images/cover.png` in `foreword.md` / title.
  - `images/ruth_fuller_sasaki_1955.png` in `editors-prologue.md`.
