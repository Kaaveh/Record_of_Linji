## Context

Part Three records the biographical events and travels (*xinglu* 行錄) of Linji:
- His initial training under Huangbo: asking three times about the buddhadharma and being beaten three times.
- His breakthrough under Dayu: realizing "there isn't so much to Huangbo's buddhadharma after all!" and punching Dayu in the ribs.
- Communal monastic labor: planting pine trees, hoeing the fields, digging the ground.
- His encounters on pilgrimage with famous Tang masters (Longguang, Sanfeng, Daci, Huayan, Fenglin, Jinniu).
- Section 68: Linji's final moments and passing away, followed by the Pagoda / Stupa Inscription (*Taming* 塔銘) composed by Yanzhao of Baoshou.
- Section 69: The historical preface by the Song court official Ma Fang (馬防, 1120), giving a soaring, poetic summary of Linji's life, shouting, and transmission.

---

## Key Challenges & Registers

1. **Narrative Biography vs. Encounter**:
   - The opening section (`47.md`) is one of the most celebrated stories in all of Zen literature. The translation must carry emotional warmth («مهربانیِ مادربزرگانه» for grandmotherly kindness) alongside spiritual vigor.
2. **The Stupa Inscription (`68.md`)**:
   - Epigraphic Tang funeral monument prose. Formal, dignified, recording family lineage, places, dates, imperial posthumous titles (Huizhao 慧照 "Illuminating Wisdom", Chengling 澄靈 "Translucent Spirit").
3. **Ma Fang's Preface (`69-ma-fang-preface.md`)**:
   - Classical Chinese 4-character and 6-character parallel prose (*pianwen*). Dense allusion to every major episode in the book.
